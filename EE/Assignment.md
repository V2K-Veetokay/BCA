1. Bhopal Gas Tragedy

The Bhopal Gas Tragedy is considered one of the world’s worst industrial disasters. It occurred on the night of December 2–3, 1984, at the Union Carbide India Limited (UCIL) pesticide plant in Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh. A highly toxic gas called Methyl Isocyanate (MIC) leaked from one of the storage tanks due to poor maintenance, faulty safety systems, and neglect of safety protocols.

The leak resulted in immediate deaths and severe injuries. Official figures reported over 3,000 deaths, but unofficial estimates suggest 15,000–20,000 people may have died over time. More than 500,000 people were exposed to the gas, leading to long-term health issues such as respiratory disorders, blindness, birth defects, cancer, and neurological damage.

The tragedy exposed major issues related to industrial safety, corporate responsibility, and government regulation. It led to stronger environmental and industrial laws in India, such as the Environment Protection Act of 1986, making companies more accountable for hazardous operations.

2. BS6 Engine Norms
 
BS6 (Bharat Stage VI) engine norms are the latest emission standards implemented by the Government of India to control air pollution from vehicles. They came into force on 1 April 2020, skipping BS5 entirely and moving directly from BS4 to BS6.

Key features of BS6 norms:

Stricter emission limits:

Up to 80% reduction in particulate matter (PM) emissions from diesel vehicles.

Significant reduction in NOx (Nitrogen Oxides) emissions (70% for diesel, 25% for petrol).

#### Cleaner fuel:

BS6 fuel contains only 10 ppm sulphur, compared to 50 ppm in BS4, reducing harmful exhaust.

Advanced technologies in vehicles:

* Diesel cars now include DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) and SCR (Selective Catalytic Reduction) systems.

* Petrol vehicles use improved catalytic converters.

* OBD (Onboard Diagnostics)

* Helps monitor real-time emissions and engine performance.

Overall, BS6 norms significantly reduce vehicular pollution and help improve India's air quality.

3.Environmental Issue Around Us: Garbage Dumping

One common environmental problem around us is garbage dumping in open areas. People often throw plastic bags, food waste, and other rubbish on streets or empty plots instead of using dustbins. This waste rots in the open, creating a bad smell and attracting insects and animals.

When it rains, the garbage gets washed into drains and water bodies, causing water pollution and clogging drains, which leads to flooding. Burning garbage also releases harmful smoke that pollutes the air.

This problem happens because of lack of awareness, irregular waste collection, and overuse of plastic. To solve it, we should use dustbins, reduce plastic use, separate waste at home, and keep our surroundings clean.
